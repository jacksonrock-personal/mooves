What is new, and what deliberately is not

No badge, no count, no dot — and this is the release most tempted to break that. A waiting request is announced by one push and then by the section simply being there. A number on the People tab would be the first unread count in the app, and the rule against them is not decoration: it is why opening Mooves does not feel like a job.

The rows are FriendRow’s geometry — 40px avatar, 13px gap, 20px gutter, 15px medium name, hairline underneath — with a reason line added and the week chip swapped for the action. A request and a friend are the same kind of object in the same list, so they should not look like two different apps.

Co-attendance outranks mutual friends, always, however many mutuals the other person has. It is the only signal that is evidence rather than inference: you were in a room together. It is also the revision to “Option B” — that was approved as an instant one-tap add on the reasoning that showing up together is consent, and it is not being built. Ana consented to attending a Moove, not to sharing when she is free. Co-attendance now changes the ORDER of the list, not the consent.

Declines are silent and final. The sender is never told and cannot ask again. A “no” that can be re-sent is not a no — and the absence of any signal back is exactly what makes declining feel free. The moment declining carries a social cost, people stop declining and start ignoring, and then the list is full of things nobody will ever resolve.

Open questions

1. “Asked” vs “Requested” vs “Pending” on the sent state. Drawn with Asked — it is what you did, in the app’s own plain voice, where the other two are statuses.

2. Does a sent request stay in the list at all? Drawn as staying, greyed, with a line saying she will see it. The alternative is it disappearing on tap, which is tidier but leaves you unsure whether it sent.

3. Decline placement. Drawn as a quiet text button LEFT of Accept, so the destructive one is not the one your thumb lands on. Worth checking on a real device.

4. Suggestions sit below the friend list, which means someone with 60 friends never scrolls to them. Above the list is more visible and more annoying. This is the one placement I am least sure about.
