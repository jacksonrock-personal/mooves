What actually fixes the discoverability problem

Not the sheet. The arrow direction and the word. A down chevron promises the card grows in place, so nothing under it is expected to be a different kind of thing. Changing it to › and labelling the row "5 in · Comments" would fix the confusion without any sheet at all — that fix is available to the inline version you already approved and shipped.

The sheet earns its keep on two other things instead: a 430-character comment stops deforming the feed, and the compose field sits on the keyboard by construction rather than by hook. Those are the real reasons to switch, and they only matter once people write long comments.

What the sheet costs

A tap becomes a context switch. Inline, the roster is a glance; in a sheet the feed is covered and you have to dismiss to get back. Checking "who's in" is the more frequent action and it gets slower so that the rarer one gets better.

Editing goes two sheets deep (see the "···" state), unless we edit in place instead.

It is a real rebuild. `PlanComments` moves out of `WhosIn`'s footer slot into a sheet component, `WhosIn` goes back to how it shipped, and the card row changes. Roughly the same size as the original build, and the migration and the API are untouched by any of it.

My recommendation

Try the two-character fix first — chevron ▾ → ›, label "5 in · Comments" — on the inline version that is already built and pushed. It is a few lines, it targets exactly the confusion you hit, and it costs nothing if you then want the sheet anyway.

Then go to Option A if long comments turn out to be common in real use. If everything people write is "running 10 late", inline is genuinely better, because a glance beats a tap.
