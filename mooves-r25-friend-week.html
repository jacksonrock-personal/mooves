Tonight, from 5

The sheet hugs its content. No scroller, no near-full-height panel — the whole week, the headline and the one button land in about half the screen, with the list you came from still visible behind it. That compactness is the strip's whole argument.

Two weights of green, not two colours. Now that a planned slot is green, green can no longer be what says "live" — so live is a solid bar and everything he merely planned is an outline on a faint fill. Toggle Green right now and watch Monday evening go solid. It is the only bar in the week that does.

What the three rows actually mean

Phase 22 ships four parts, and weekdays and weekends do not offer the same ones: a weekday has Day (9–5) and Evening (5–11); a weekend has Morning (8–12), Afternoon (12–5) and Evening (5–11). There is no weekday morning, deliberately — see partsForWeekday.

So the top row is empty Monday through Friday and that is correct, not a gap: the cell is not drawn at all rather than drawn hollow, because "he has no Monday morning" and "there is no such thing as a Monday morning" are different statements. Same rule slotColumns() already applies to your own week.

You asked for the rows spelled Morning / Day / Evening, so the middle row is labelled "Day" and on Saturday and Sunday it is holding an Afternoon slot. Toggle to Michael's week and look at Sat: all three rows are filled, and the middle one is his afternoon. That is the one place the spelled-out label is slightly generous, and it is a fair trade for not inventing a fourth row that is blank five days a week.

The privacy rules — decided

Availability is structurally private today: there is no route anywhere in the codebase that returns another user's slots, and /api/availability says so in as many words. This feature reverses that deliberately, under two rules:

1. Confirmed friends only, on by default, no setting. Same rule as the rail. If a friend can already see you go green, seeing that you planned Thursday evening is not a bigger disclosure — and a toggle most people never find would make the feature unreliable for everyone else.

2. Group scoping applies. A green scoped away from you never reaches your rail, and a week scoped away from you must never reach this sheet either. That makes the read path a SQL function carrying the same scope check, not a plain table select — so the rule is structural rather than a filter somebody has to remember to apply, exactly as it is for greens today.

The consequence to build around:

Because scoping applies, "4 this week" on the Friends row is a scoped count, not a raw one. Two people looking at the same friend can honestly see different numbers, and the aggregate behind that chip has to run through the same function rather than counting rows. Cheap to do now, expensive to retrofit.

And the disclosure needs a home. You cut the footer line, so this sheet no longer says who can see a week. That is defensible — the honest place to say it is the ritual sheet, at the moment someone sets the slots, not the screen where they read someone else's. It just has to land there.

Two things the strip gives up, so they are on the record

Day-of-month numbers. Seven columns on a 320px phone leaves each day about 29px — enough for "MON", not for "MON / 4". The date range in the header (Mon 4 – Sun 10 Aug) is now carrying that on its own. Say the word and I'll put a 8.5px date under each day letter; it fits, it just adds noise.

Slot names inside the cells. The grid could write "Evening" in the filled cell; the strip cannot, so the row label on the left is the only thing naming a part. That is exactly why spelling them out in full was the right call.

Why "Plan something" is gone, and what would bring it back

It would have opened the composer with the slot pre-filled and Michael tagged. The problem is the word "the slot" — the strip has no selection. Nothing in this sheet says which of his four free windows you are planning around, so the button could only ever have opened the composer with Michael tagged, and the cow button already does that from anywhere. It was a second CTA buying almost nothing. Cut, agreed.

What would earn it back is making the bars themselves the affordance: tap Thursday evening, get a Moove dated Thursday evening with Michael on it. That is the real feature, and the strip is a better home for it than a button ever was — the cell already knows which slot it is. It also turns a read-only screen into a write one, which is its own spec, its own privacy question ("he can see I looked"), and not something to smuggle in here.

One more thing, worth saying out loud

The Friends-row chip means the Friends list has to load everyone's counts — one extra aggregate query. Cheap, and it is the difference between a screen that lists names and a screen that answers a question.
